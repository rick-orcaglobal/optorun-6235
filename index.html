TSE Prime 6235 · FY Dec · optical thin-film deposition equipment Research cut-off 21 Oct 2026 · private note
The takeaway

Optorun looks cheap, but it is not yet a buy. The market pays little for the coating business, yet the AI order surge has not reached profits, history says such waves fade fast, and the assets that make the stock cheap sit where minority shareholders may not reach them. Watchlist; the next test is the Q3 gross margin on 10 November.

The one question that matters: is the AI optical-communications surge a new, durable earnings curve for Optorun, or another equipment cycle?

Six points

  • Cheap on paper. Strip out the listed SeeYA stake (~¥33bn after tax) and cash, and the market values the coating business at roughly ¥29bn, or 3.6× FY2017-25 average operating profit DERIVED.
  • Orders up, profits flat. H1 FY2026 orders rose 75.7% year on year; revenue rose 14.5% and operating profit fell 1.9% DERIVED.
  • History reads as a cycle, not a curve. Each past growth wave rested on one anchor customer whose purchases then fell by around 35% a year; the stock last peaked at an order peak, in January 2018, and fell 64% from there to the end of that year DERIVED.
  • Margin is the swing factor. FY2026 guidance needs an H2 gross margin of around 41%, against 32~34% for six quarters running. Management has forecast revenue to within around a tenth, but not the margin DERIVED.
  • The cheapness is hard to reach. 68% of group cash sat outside the parent at FY2025 year-end, and the stake sits in a Shanghai subsidiary under a lock-up to around March 2027, with no stated plan DERIVED IR.
  • The moat is not yet evidenced. No filing names a competitor, a market share or pricing; why customers choose Optorun, and whether that carries into optical communications, is the largest open question ABSENT.

1. What Optorun sells, and what is driving it

One product, one country, and a new AI-related leg that is now doing the work.

  • Optorun sells vacuum deposition tools that apply optical coatings to lenses, filters and sensors. Revenue is booked on customer acceptance (検収), so the P&L follows installations rather than orders FILED.
  • Demand has always been driven by feature content rather than handset units: multi-lens cameras, ADAS, head-up displays and, since FY2025, the optical interconnect inside AI data centres FILED.
  • Optical-communications order guidance for FY2026 was raised from around ¥5bn to ¥20bn on enquiries from transceiver makers serving US hyperscalers, and 2Q optical-component orders rose 249% year on year IR.
  • China took 85.9% of FY2025 revenue (90.9% with Taiwan), production sits in two leased Shanghai plants plus Taiwan, and at least three-quarters of staff work in Shanghai DERIVED. None of the last three 有価証券報告書 carries a US-China risk factor ABSENT.

3. New growth curve or another equipment cycle?

On the evidence so far, this looks more like a cycle: strong now, but past waves have faded quickly and the stock has topped at order peaks before.

  • The mechanics. Optorun's orders track how much capacity its customers are adding, not how much they sell. End demand can keep growing after equipment orders peak.
  • The base rate. Each past wave rested on one anchor customer: Apple (55.2% of FY2017 sales; ¥18.4bn falling to ¥2.2bn by FY2022), then Zhejiang Sunny (¥8.2bn in FY2022 to ¥3.7bn in FY2024), then Largan (¥5.8bn in FY2024, below ¥3.4bn in FY2025). After the peak year, purchases fell by around 35% a year (n = 3 waves) DERIVED.
  • Customer spending does not pass through. Largan raised capex 11% in FY2025 while Optorun's revenue from Largan fell by at least 42% DERIVED. Crystal-Optech, the one customer that discloses its purchases from Optorun, spent a mean 25% of its capex with Optorun in 2017-19 and 11% in 2022-25 DERIVED.
  • The current wave has not peaked. Customers' H1 2026 capex was 2.8× the prior year for Chinese component makers, 3.7× for Chinese transceiver makers and 3.2× for Coherent and Lumentum DERIVED. That capex moves with Optorun's orders rather than ahead of them (levels r 0.92, growth r 0.2~0.3, n = 14 / 10), so it will confirm the peak rather than predict it DERIVED.
  • The price precedent. FY2017 orders of ¥52.5bn were the record. The stock listed in December 2017, peaked at ¥4,705 a month later and closed 2018 at ¥1,680, in the year revenue hit its high. The 2026 peak, ¥4,820 on 8 May, also came at an order peak.
  • What would make it a curve. Repeat orders across several customer cohorts, attractive margins on the new orders, and evidence that customers choose Optorun for reasons that carry across technology generations. None of the three is yet shown.

4. Margins decide the earnings

Volume is coming; whether it carries enough margin is the open question, and management's own record on margin is poor.

  • Gross margin stepped down to 32.1% in 2024Q4 and has not exceeded 38.5% in any quarter since IR. High-margin ALD tools fell from 34% of revenue in FY2023 to 3% in FY2025, which explains FY2025, but not the initial step: ALD was still 36% of revenue in 2024Q4 IR.
  • Over three years (n = 3), operating-profit guidance missed by +13%, −12% and −56%; revenue guidance by +4%, −12% and −11% DERIVED.
  • FY2026 guidance (¥6.2bn) requires ¥5.1bn of operating profit in H2 against ¥1.1bn in H1. With H2 revenue at the guided ¥22.3bn and costs flat on H1, the H2 gross margin determines the outcome DERIVED:

5. Cheap, but can shareholders reach the value?

The value is real on paper, but it sits in Shanghai subsidiaries and a locked-up stake, and nothing forces it out.

  • SeeYA stake. Held by 光馳科技(上海), 100% owned, so no minorities share the revaluation FILED. The company attributes ¥34.6bn of the OCI reserve to it at RMB 51.3 on 30 June IR; the last confirmed close is RMB 45.49 (688781.SS, 20 Oct 2026), around 11% lower. Lock-up to around March 2027, stated only in the deck IR.
  • Cash. ¥39.1bn at 30 June against ¥5.4bn of debt, 99.85% of which is a put-carrying instrument in another Shanghai subsidiary (4.8%, due 2029) FILED. ¥12.5bn of the cash arrived as customer prepayments in H1 DERIVED. ¥21.3bn (68%) of group cash sat outside the parent at FY2025 year-end DERIVED, although subsidiaries did pay ¥6.21bn (FY2023) and ¥0.82bn (FY2025) of dividends up to the parent FILED.
  • Capital policy. 10.1% of shares sit in treasury, uncancelled; two buybacks returned ¥8.9bn; the dividend is ¥54 (FY2026E ¥56). Buyback language is "will consider", the ROE ≥10% target was missed in two of the last three years (5.1% in FY2025), and there is no PBR target FILED.

Bullish, but is it tradeable?

Not yet: a watchlist name until the margin shows up or the company acts on the stake or the treasury shares.

  • The ingredients are there: a heavily discounted listed asset, a near-record backlog, a founder who sold 1.2m shares at ¥3,700 in May with 58.5% of the remainder pledged, a July 2028 TOPIX deadline, and uncancelled treasury stock.
  • What is missing is a mechanism. The largest holder (浙江水晶光電, 16.3%) is also a customer, the 51% JV partner in 晶馳 and holds a board seat FILED; no activist is on the register, and every large holder files a passive purpose.
  • The two tests to watch: an H2 gross margin heading towards 41%, and any plan for the stake or the treasury shares.

How the stake value is built

Investment securities rose from ¥4.18bn to ¥47.06bn over H1 FY2026. Netting off the other holdings (AI Mechatec at its ¥3.33bn cost per the deck, 筑波精工 and two unlisted lines) gives a SeeYA carrying value of around ¥43.2bn, struck at the company's own 30 June price of RMB 51.3 DERIVED. The calculator scales that figure linearly with the SeeYA price and the CNYJPY move, and then deducts tax on the gain. As the OCI line is already net of tax in the accounts, the filed reserve (¥34.6bn attributed by the company) sits below the gross mark. AI Mechatec is taken at ¥3.33bn, its 30 June carrying value per the deck (FY2025 year-end fair value: ¥5.43bn), and other 出資金, including the 49% stake in 晶馳, at the ¥6.70bn book figure.

Why "no controlling shareholder" undersells the entanglement

  • The largest holder's own chairman sits on Optorun's board as an outside director but is not filed as independent, and two of five directors are TSE-notified independents (S100XTSL p.44-51).
  • No transaction amount with Crystal-Optech has been disclosed in any year, with the related-party categories omitted as immaterial.
  • The one disclosed constraint runs against Optorun, which may not run deposition production identical to the JV's output (S100XTSL p.18).
  • A Chinese fund holds 10.4% of 光馳半導体技術(上海), with a put on undisclosed triggers, and supplies a director to the group; the fund absorbed around 95% of FY2025 group interest expense.
  • The founder family's filings are lodged through Optorun's own corporate-planning department.

What would change the reading

Towards a position

  • Repeat optical-communications orders across several customers, with gross margin on track for the ~41% H2 needs.
  • A turn back up in the rolling SeeYA beta from 0.18 while SeeYA holds around RMB 45.
  • A recovery in Q3 gross margin to above 38%, with optical-comms revenue starting to convert.
  • A stated plan for the SeeYA stake after lock-up, a new buyback, or a treasury cancellation as a signal of intent.
  • The appearance of a non-passive filer on the register.

Away from one

  • Customer capex growth slowing below 1.5× year on year for two quarters, the signal that the order wave has peaked.
  • A move in SeeYA back towards its July low (RMB 34.5), or a disclosed sale restriction beyond March 2027.
  • A full-year operating-profit outcome at the bottom of the ¥2.7~7.0bn calibration range, with H2 margin stuck near 32%.
  • Evidence that the cash sits mostly in China and is not distributable upstream.
  • Further founder disposals or a margin call on the pledged block.

Questions we cannot yet answer

  1. Why do customers choose Optorun? Yield, throughput, uptime, service or cost per good part; and whether that edge carries into optical communications. No competitor, share or pricing data is disclosed.
  2. What margin do the new orders carry? Backlog by application and delivery date, and gross margin by product line, are not disclosed.
  3. Is the demand one capacity build or repeat business? New capacity, replacement and upgrades are not separated.
  4. How much of the asset value can reach shareholders? SeeYA share count and tax basis, cash by entity, and the put triggers on the subsidiary instrument are undisclosed.

Disclosure gaps

  • The SeeYA share count, holding and lock-up appear only in the IR deck; the deck's 30 June price (RMB 51.3) differs from the market-data close (51.03), an immaterial 0.5%.
  • The H1 FY2026 tax charge exceeded pre-tax profit (114.6%), with no reconciliation in the filings.
  • The Quzhou fund's put triggers, strike and formula are undisclosed.
  • No geographic split of cash or total assets, and no backlog-conversion schedule.
  • The IR deck that carries the quarterly series and the mid-term plan has no durable URL.
